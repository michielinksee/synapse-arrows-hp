How Japanese AI search cites local businesses: six industries, four AIs, 1,500+ answers (October 2026)

10 November 2026 · Synapse Arrows · Research carried out October 2026 in Sapporo and Fukuoka, Japan

About this article: Written by Synapse Arrows, which sells AI visibility work (AI映え診断) and operates the independent measurement instrument KanseiLINK. The numbers are counts of AI answers and the sources they cited; they are not ratings or rankings of any business. AI answers change with wording, date and account. No business is named. This is the English summary of seven Japanese articles published on 2 November 2026; the per-industry detail and charts are in Japanese, linked below.

There is no single "AI search optimization" in Japan, because the place an AI goes to read differs by industry.

More people in Japan now ask ChatGPT and its peers "Which hair salon in Sapporo is good?" or "Which tax accountant handles inheritance?" (in an August 2026 CyberAgent survey of 9,278 people, 52.3% of respondents said they use generative AI for search). Where does the AI read to write that answer? In October 2026 we put questions about six industries in Sapporo and Fukuoka to four AIs and counted, industry by industry, the pages each answer cited as its sources.

The results differ sharply by industry. If you work on GEO or AEO in English-speaking markets, or you are a company considering Japan, the short version is: the sources you are used to (review platforms, LinkedIn, G2, Google Maps) are not what Japanese-language AI answers are built from.

What we found
  • Restaurants: the AI top ten and the Google top ten are different lists. In 5 of 7 dining situations, not one restaurant appeared in both. 12 of the 20 restaurants the AIs named most often were described with details that did not match the official information.
  • Tax accountants: 54.5% of cited pages were the firms' own websites. The more specific the question, the more the firms' own pages were read instead of comparison sites.
  • Hotels: the booking sites the AI reads swap with the language of the question. Japanese: Ikyu.com, JTB, Yahoo! Travel. English: Trip.com, Expedia, Booking.com.
  • Beauty salons: 91 of 100 answers cited Hot Pepper Beauty. Google Maps: 0.
  • Real estate agents: 66 of 100 answers cited a "ranking" or "top N recommended" article. Half of all cited pages were such articles.
  • IT and web development: 69 of 100 answers cited a "top N" article. LinkedIn, GitHub, Crunchbase and G2: 0.

Method, in brief

  • AIs: ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Perplexity, each with web search on.
  • Questions: built from the words people actually use to search for each industry (Google's search suggestions for Sapporo and Fukuoka), in two forms: a short question and a longer request with context. Hotels and restaurants were also asked in English, from a foreign traveller's point of view, to compare languages.
  • Counting: for each answer, the sites it cited as sources, de-duplicated per answer. Where Gemini gave redirect links we followed them to the destination site. Gemini often shows no sources at all.
  • Dates: 2, 20 and 28 October 2026 (per-industry detail in the Japanese articles).

Three patterns

Pattern 1: booking platforms (restaurants, beauty salons, hotels)

The AIs go to the large booking platforms where every business has a structured page. For restaurants that is Tabelog (食べログ); for salons, Hot Pepper Beauty (ホットペッパービューティー); for hotels, Ikyu (一休) and Jalan (じゃらん) in Japanese, and Trip.com, Expedia and Booking.com in English. In these industries, whether your own page on the platform is up to date is the first thing that matters. For beauty salons, 23 of 100 answers cited a specific salon's page on a booking platform rather than a listing. Google Maps and Instagram, where customers do look, were almost never read when the AI composed its answer.

Pattern 2: the business's own pages (tax accountants)

For tax accountants, the firms' own websites were read more than comparison sites (54.5% of cited pages). What gets read is not the home page but pages that take a customer's question as their title and contain numbers: "how to choose a tax accountant for inheritance", "what a monthly retainer costs". For a vague question ("recommended tax accountants?"), comparison sites made up 58% of sources. For specific topics the firms' own pages passed 60%: inheritance 62%, start-ups 63%, bookkeeping 75%. In a separate nationwide set (12 questions, 48 answers, 30 September 2026), 10 of the 13 firm pages cited used the question's wording in the title, 11 contained figures, and 9 named a licensed accountant as author or supervisor.

Pattern 3: listicles (real estate, IT and web development)

For real estate agents and IT companies, "ranking" and "top N recommended" articles made up about half of all cited pages (51% and 55%). The authors were not only comparison media: many were competitors publishing a "top N companies in Sapporo" list on their own site. In these industries, the first step is to check whether the articles the AIs are reading describe your company correctly. We are not recommending paid placement in such articles, and we did not test whether paid placement gets a company cited.

What was true in every industry

  • A business the AI does not name is not in the comparison set. It is not ranked lower; it is treated as if it did not exist.
  • Each AI reads different places. Within the same industry the mix of sources changes between ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini. Gemini often shows no sources. For IT companies, Perplexity cited Hatchu Navi in 10 of 25 answers and ChatGPT in none.
  • The same question gets a different answer each time. For tax accountants, the overlap in firms named across three runs ranged from 17% to 59% depending on the AI. Testing once and concluding "we appear" or "we don't" is unreliable.
  • Official websites are read. In every industry the AIs fetched the business's own site. For hotels, official sites were cited in 52% to 69% of answers in both languages. Prices, menus and services written as text, not images, have a reason to exist.

Why this matters outside Japan

If you measure AI visibility for a client entering Japan, or you are that company, the English-language playbook does not transfer as it is. The sources that build a Japanese-language answer are Japanese platforms: Tabelog, Hot Pepper Beauty, Ikyu, Jalan, Yahoo! Travel, SUUMO, LIFULL HOME'S, the accountant directories run by freee, Money Forward and Yayoi, and Japanese vendor directories such as Hatchu Navi, PRONI Aimitsu and Web Kanji. In 100 Japanese answers about choosing a developer, LinkedIn, GitHub, Crunchbase and G2 were cited zero times; ITreview (a Japanese review site) 6 times and Clutch 2. The hotel data shows the same thing from the other side: ask the same question in English and the booking sites swap to Trip.com, Expedia, TripAdvisor and Booking.com. We wrote a separate guide on what this means for a company entering Japan: Getting cited by Japanese AI search.

Frequently asked questions

Is AI search optimization the same for every industry in Japan?

No. In this October 2026 measurement, restaurants, beauty salons and hotels were cited mainly from booking sites, tax accountants from the firms' own pages, and real estate and IT companies from top-N recommendation articles. Check where the AIs read for your industry before deciding what to fix first.

If my Google Maps and Instagram are in good shape, will Japanese AI search cite me?

Not in this study. For beauty salons, 0 of 100 answers cited Google Maps and 1 cited Instagram; for hotels, 0 of 100 answers cited Google Maps. Where customers search and where the AI reads when it writes an answer are different places.

I asked an AI once and my company did not appear. Does that mean I am not listed?

Not necessarily. When the same tax accountant question was put to the same AI three times, the set of firms named overlapped by 59% for Perplexity, 42% for Gemini, 40% for Claude and 17% for ChatGPT. One run is not enough to judge.

Do Japanese AIs cite LinkedIn, G2 or Crunchbase for IT companies?

In 100 Japanese-language answers about choosing a web or software developer, LinkedIn, GitHub, Crunchbase and G2 were cited zero times. Japanese vendor directories and top-N articles were cited instead.

Limits of this research

Only restaurants and hotels were measured in two cities (Sapporo and Fukuoka). Tax accountants, beauty salons, real estate and IT were measured in Sapporo alone (the IT set includes nationwide questions), and beauty salons, real estate and IT are one run per AI. Results change with city and date.

"Where the AI reads" counts the sites an answer cited as sources. We did not check whether those sites' information is correct or how the AI used it, except for the restaurant fact-check of 142 statements about 20 restaurants.
