Getting cited by Japanese AI search: a guide for companies entering Japan (2026)

10 November 2026 · Synapse Arrows

About this article: Written by Synapse Arrows, which sells AI visibility work (AI映え診断) in Japan and Singapore and operates the independent measurement instrument KanseiLINK. The evidence comes from our own measurement of six industries in October 2026, summarised in English here. Our Japanese guide on the same subject is written for Japanese companies going abroad; this one is the mirror image, for companies coming in.

If you sell in Japan, your customers increasingly ask ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity or Google's AI Overviews in Japanese, and the answer is built from Japanese sources. An English website, G2 reviews and a LinkedIn page do not carry over: in 100 Japanese-language answers about choosing a software or web developer, LinkedIn, GitHub, Crunchbase and G2 were cited zero times. This guide sets out what cross-border AI visibility means when the target market is Japan, and the five steps to get there, with the numbers behind each step.

1. Why English-language work does not get you cited in Japanese answers

Generative AI changes where it reads according to the language of the question. Asked in Japanese, it reads Japanese pages, Japanese platforms and Japanese mentions. However strong your English-language presence, if the Japanese information space holds few traces of your company, the Japanese answer will not include you. Three things are separate:

  • The sources are different. For hotels in Sapporo and Fukuoka, the same question asked in Japanese and English produced hotel lists that overlapped by only 47% and 51%. The booking sites the AI read swapped from Ikyu.com, JTB and Yahoo! Travel (Japanese) to Trip.com, Expedia, TripAdvisor and Booking.com (English).
  • The entity is split. If the Japanese rendering of your company and product names (katakana, or a romanised form) is not tied to the English entity, the AI treats them as different things and your track record does not accumulate.
  • The third-party signals are different. For developers, the Japanese answers cited the vendor directories Hatchu Navi (15 of 100 answers), PRONI Aimitsu (14) and Web Kanji (12), the Japanese review site ITreview (6), and Clutch (2). Not LinkedIn, GitHub, Crunchbase or G2.

2. The gap between home-market tools and Japanese agencies

A company entering Japan usually has two kinds of help within reach. AI visibility platforms from the US and Europe are built around English queries and English sources; whether they measure Japanese questions against Japanese platforms, and how, is something to confirm with each vendor. Japanese AEO and LLMO agencies work in Japanese for the domestic market and are not always set up to brief an overseas head office in English. The company sits between the two: it needs the Japanese measurement, and it needs to understand the result in its own language. We compared the tools on each side in a separate article, AI visibility tools for the Japanese market: 12 compared.

3. What cross-border AI visibility is

Cross-border AI visibility is how correctly and how often generative AI cites and recommends your company to a given country and language. It is a separate thing from your home-market visibility: you measure it separately and fix it separately, market by market and language by language.

4. Five steps to being cited in Japanese

  1. Measure where you stand, with Japanese questions

    Build the questions from the words Japanese customers use (Google's Japanese search suggestions are a practical source), in two forms: a short question and a longer request with context. Put them to ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Perplexity with web search on, and look at Google AI Overviews. Run each question more than once: for tax accountants, the same question put three times to the same AI returned firm lists that overlapped by 59% (Perplexity), 42% (Gemini), 40% (Claude) and 17% (ChatGPT). One run tells you little.

  2. Create Japanese primary information

    Not a machine-translated copy of the English site. In every industry we measured, the AIs fetched the business's own website; for hotels, official sites were cited in 52% to 69% of answers. What got cited were pages that take a customer's question as their title and contain numbers. Of 13 tax-accountant pages cited in a nationwide set, 10 used the question's wording in the title, 11 contained figures, and 9 named a licensed professional as author or supervisor. Write prices, specifications and what you can and cannot do as text, not as images.

  3. Align the entity

    Fix one Japanese rendering of the company and product names and use it everywhere. Tie it to the English entity with structured data (Organization, Product, FAQ) and with links between your official site and your pages on Japanese platforms. The hotel data shows what happens otherwise: a hotel written differently across booking sites can be counted as a different hotel.

  5. Re-measure and look at the difference

    Repeat the same Japanese questions on the same AIs after the changes, and compare both whether you appear and how you are described. For restaurants, 12 of the 20 businesses the AIs named most often were described with details that did not match the official information (non-existent branches, wrong prices, wrong opening hours), so appearing is not the end of the job. Measurement by a third party, separate from whoever does the implementation, makes the before-and-after comparison easier to trust.

5. How to measure in Japanese

  • Separate Japanese queries. A high citation rate in English says nothing about Japanese. Prepare question sets per market and language.
  • Decide which AIs. The ones your Japanese customers use: ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Overviews, plus Claude and Perplexity. Their sources differ. For developers, Perplexity cited Hatchu Navi in 10 of 25 answers and ChatGPT in none; Gemini showed sources in only 14 of 25.
  • Look at how you are described, not only whether you appear: category, strengths, prices, locations.
  • Use a third-party measurement alongside any agency's own report.

For scale: in an August 2026 CyberAgent survey of 9,278 people in Japan, 52.3% of respondents said they use generative AI for search. The AI answer is becoming an entry point in Japan as elsewhere.

6. Common mistakes

  • Machine-translating the English site and stopping there. Weak as Japanese primary information; rarely cited.
  • Measuring only in English and concluding from the English numbers that Japan is covered.
  • Inconsistent Japanese spellings of the company or product name, which split the entity.
  • No Japanese third-party mentions. Your own site alone is a thin trust signal, and in three of the six industries the AIs read booking platforms or listicles before anything else.
  • Judging from one run. The same question returns different businesses on different runs.

7. Industry notes

  • SaaS and IT: Japanese "recommended N" articles and Japanese vendor directories are where developers were cited; G2 and Crunchbase were not. Fix the Japanese category name for your product and make sure the Japanese articles that list your category describe you correctly.
  • Hospitality and inbound: be the same hotel on the Japanese booking sites (Ikyu.com, Jalan, Rakuten Travel, Yahoo! Travel) and the English ones, and put rooms, prices, check-in times and directions on the official site as text. Our hotel article has the tables.
  • Professional services: the AIs read the firm's own pages when the question is specific. One Japanese page per topic, titled in the customer's words, with figures and a named professional.
  • Consumer services and retail: the Japanese booking and reservation platforms for your category come first; Google Maps and Instagram were almost never cited.

8. Frequently asked questions

Do we need a Japanese website to be cited by Japanese AI search?

In practice, yes. Japanese-language answers are built from Japanese sources, and in every industry we measured the AIs fetched the business's own site. A machine-translated copy of the English site is weak as primary information; the pages that were cited answered a customer's question in its own words and contained figures.

Does our English-language AI visibility count for nothing in Japan?

It is not wasted, but it is a separate thing. The sources differ by language: for hotels, the same question in Japanese and English produced lists that overlapped by 47% to 51% and read different booking sites. Measure and fix each market and language separately.

Which AIs should we measure for Japan?

The ones your Japanese customers use: ChatGPT, Gemini, Google AI Overviews, and also Claude and Perplexity. Each reads different places. In our IT measurement, Perplexity cited the vendor directory Hatchu Navi in 10 of 25 answers and ChatGPT in none.

How is this different from domestic AEO in Japan?

The work is the same kind, but a company entering Japan starts without Japanese primary information, without a fixed Japanese rendering of its name, and without the Japanese third-party mentions that answers are built from. Those three gaps come first.

Being cited by Japanese AI search takes its own loop: measure in Japanese, create Japanese primary information, align the entity, earn the Japanese third-party mentions your industry uses, and re-measure. The single biggest mistake is judging Japan from English-language numbers.
