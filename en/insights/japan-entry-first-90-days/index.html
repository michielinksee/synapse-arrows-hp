After the market study: your first 90 days in Japan

16 November 2026 · Synapse Arrows

About this article: Written by Synapse Arrows, which sells Japan market-entry support to companies from Singapore and the region. The last section describes our own service. Facts were checked against the sources listed at the end on 16 November 2026.

Synapse Arrows is a Singapore-incorporated company whose founder is based in Japan. We help companies from Singapore and the region set up in Japan and win their first Japanese customers.

This article is for founders and business development leads who have finished a market study of Japan, or who already have a Japanese prospect asking for a proposal. You know the market is there. The question now is what to do in the next 90 days, and in what order.

The short version
  • A Japanese subsidiary is not always needed for a first cross-border sale. Registration and licensing requirements depend on your activities, and customer requirements also affect the choice.
  • Registration processing can take weeks after application. Bank-account opening can also affect the schedule, so start it early.
  • Japanese law does not require the representative director of a KK to live in Japan. Check each bank's and landlord's requirements.
  • Start customer conversations in the first month. The entity work runs alongside them.

Days 1 to 30: start customer conversations while you plan the entity

A Japanese buyer that takes a first meeting with an overseas company will want to know who they will sign with, who will support them in Japanese, and what happens if something goes wrong.

Three things to do in the first month:

  1. Pick ten named accounts. Use the list from your market study. Ten companies you can name are more useful than a segment.
  2. Put your core material into Japanese. That means a product page, a two-page proposal, and a security and data-handling sheet, written so that the buyer can circulate them internally.
  3. Decide how the first contract will be signed. Three routes to compare for a first cross-border sale:
RouteWho signs with the customerFits when
Direct from SingaporeYour Singapore companyThe customer is comfortable with an overseas contract and paying across borders
Japanese resellerThe Japanese reseller, in its own nameThe customer's procurement requires a domestic contract in yen
Employer of recordYou sign; a provider employs your Japan hireYou need one person on the ground before you have an entity

These routes are for a first cross-border sale. JETRO notes that a foreign company carrying out continuous transactions in Japan must register in Japan, and some activities need a licence; an employer of record does not change that. Confirm with a lawyer where that line falls for your business. Then ask your first two or three prospects which route their procurement team accepts. Together, the two answers tell you how soon you need the entity.

Days 31 to 60: set up the entity and start the bank process

Choosing the form

JETRO lists three options for a foreign company: a representative office, a branch, or a subsidiary. A subsidiary, either a kabushiki kaisha (KK) or a godo kaisha (GK), is one option for selling.

Time and cost

JETRO says the set-up procedures typically take about three months in total. It gives an indicative starting point of around JPY 250,000 for registering a corporation, in a cost model that uses a KK; GK costs differ. Professional fees are usually JPY 200,000 to 500,000 on top.

The resident director question

Since 16 March 2015, the Ministry of Justice has accepted registration of a KK whose representative directors all live outside Japan. JETRO states that the residence requirement likewise does not apply to the representative members of a GK, while the representative of a branch still needs an address in Japan. So for a subsidiary the law does not stop you. A director in Japan can make the bank account, the office lease and day-to-day signing easier; check each bank's and landlord's requirements.

The bank account

Start this step early. JETRO notes that each bank screens applications on its own criteria, does not disclose them, and may decline. Expect to explain your business, your beneficial owners, the purpose of the account and where your first revenue will come from. Mizuho, SMBC and MUFG each run a consultation line for foreign-affiliated subsidiaries. Prepare one clear pack in Japanese and take it to more than one bank.

The investment filing

A foreign company that sets up a Japanese subsidiary files under the Foreign Exchange and Foreign Trade Act. JETRO describes two cases: businesses in industries related to national security, public order or public safety submit a prior notification to the Bank of Japan, and other businesses submit a report within 45 days of establishment as a general rule. The Bank of Japan's guidance adds that a prior notification is itself followed by a report after the investment is carried out. Software and AI companies should confirm which case applies to them before they incorporate, not after.

If a founder plans to move to Japan

JETRO lists capital of JPY 30 million or more as one of the requirements for the Business Manager status of residence. Check the Immigration Services Agency's current criteria before you plan around a founder relocating. A Japan-resident director or a local hire may be an alternative.

A free desk while you set up

JETRO offers qualifying foreign companies a free temporary office for up to 50 business days in Tokyo, Yokohama, Nagoya, Osaka, Kobe and Fukuoka. It also introduces judicial scriveners, attorneys, tax accountants and labour consultants.

Days 61 to 90: work towards the first customer and make it repeatable

By now you should have live conversations with three to five of your ten accounts. The goal for this month is one signed contract or one paid pilot.

  • Agree the pilot in writing. Scope, length, price, and what result turns it into a full contract.
  • Set up yen invoicing. Once the account is open, you can invoice in yen from the Japanese entity if the customer's procurement asks for it.
  • Name a Japanese-speaking contact. Buyers want one person they can call. This can be a hire, a director, or a partner acting for you.
  • Write down what worked. Which title replied, which document they asked for, how long each step took. Use what you learn to refine the approach for the next customers.

Paying for it: grants on the Singapore side

Enterprise Singapore's Market Readiness Assistance (MRA) grant ceased for new applications on 29 September 2026. From 30 September 2026, applications go through the EDGE Grant.

Enterprise Singapore states that the EDGE Grant supports up to 70% of costs for SMEs and up to 50% for other companies, with support levels that differ by activity, and up to SGD 100,000 in total grant support per year across all activities. The grant is paid as a reimbursement.

Before you sign with any vendor, check the current activity list and conditions on the Enterprise Singapore site. The list decides whether incorporation advice, in-market business development or marketing can be claimed, and the conditions decide when you must apply.

On the Japan side, Enterprise Singapore and the Tokyo Metropolitan Government signed a memorandum of understanding at SWITCH 2026 to support Singapore startups expanding into Japan. Japanese cities also run their own programmes for foreign startups. Kitakyushu, for example, supported the Singapore eldercare startup SoundEye, which set up its Japanese company there in April 2025.
