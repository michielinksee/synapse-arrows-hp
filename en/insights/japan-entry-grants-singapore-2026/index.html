Grants and public support for a Singapore company entering Japan (2026)

16 November 2026 · Synapse Arrows

About this article: Written by Synapse Arrows, which sells Japan market-entry support to companies from Singapore and the region. The last section describes our own service. Every figure below comes from the official pages listed at the end, read on 16 November 2026. Grant terms change, so confirm them with the source before you act.

Synapse Arrows is a Singapore-incorporated company whose founder is based in Japan. We help companies from Singapore and the region set up in Japan and work towards their first Japanese customers.

This article is for founders and finance leads who are costing a move into Japan. It covers what Singapore pays for, what Japan offers, the conditions that decide whether you qualify, and the order to apply in. For the work itself, see your first 90 days in Japan.

The short version
  • The Market Readiness Assistance (MRA) grant ceased for new applications on 29 September 2026. The EDGE Grant replaced it from 30 September 2026.
  • EDGE's Market Entry Setup activity supports professional service costs for setting up in a target market, at up to 70% for SMEs, within a SGD 100,000 annual cap across all EDGE activities.
  • You must apply before you start work or pay a vendor, and you need at least 30% Singaporean or PR ownership.
  • Japan's support for a foreign company includes free services from JETRO and local incentives that differ by prefecture and city.

Singapore side: the EDGE Grant

What changed

Enterprise Singapore states that the Enterprise Development Grant, the MRA grant and the Productivity Solutions Grant ceased on 29 September 2026, and that from 30 September businesses apply under the EDGE Grant. Its FAQ adds that EDGE is for new projects: a company that received MRA support can apply, but not for the same project, and past MRA amounts do not count towards the EDGE cap.

Support level and cap

For internationalisation activities, Enterprise Singapore gives the support level as up to 70% for SMEs and up to 50% for other companies. Total grant support is up to SGD 100,000 per year across all EDGE activities, and the cap refreshes on 1 April each year. The FAQ says the cap is drawn down when a project is approved, not when it is claimed.

Note the difference from the old scheme. MRA's cap was SGD 100,000 per company per new market. EDGE's cap is per year and is shared with any other EDGE activity your company applies for.

What Market Entry Setup covers

The Market Entry Setup page lists six areas of support: entity incorporation, tax advisory, import and export licences, intellectual property, drafting of agreements, and trade credit insurance. It supports professional service costs. It does not support travel, accommodation or staff-related costs, GST, investment activities such as capital raising, or activities aimed at the domestic market.

The EDGE FAQ names other internationalisation activities as well, including overseas trade fairs, overseas business development staff deployment and identification of potential overseas partners. We could not read the terms of those activities on the public pages. If you plan to claim outsourced business development in Japan, ask Enterprise Singapore which activity applies before you commit.

Who qualifies

  • Ownership. The business must be registered and operating in Singapore, with at least 30% ownership, direct or indirect, by Singaporeans or Singapore permanent residents, determined by ultimate individual ownership.
  • SME level. The FAQ defines an SME by group annual sales turnover of not more than SGD 100 million, or group employment of not more than 200 workers.
  • The Japanese entity. The deliverables for entity incorporation include a shareholding record showing that the applicant holds more than 50% of the foreign entity, or the maximum allowed where foreign ownership is restricted.

Rules that catch people out

  • Apply first. The Market Entry Setup page requires that you have not started work or made any payment or deposit to a vendor before you submit. The FAQ says you may buy after submitting and before approval, at your own risk, because submission does not guarantee approval.
  • Generally, one application per activity per country. Japan needs its own application. The FAQ gives one exception: an international intellectual property filing under a system such as Madrid or PCT may cover several markets.
  • Vendor documents. The application asks for the vendor's proposal and quotation, and the vendor's track record in the target market, along with your one-year sales and job projections.
  • Apply yourself. Applications go through the Business Grants Portal, and third parties may not apply or claim on your behalf.
  • Finish in 18 months. The project must be completed within 18 months of approval.
  • You pay first. EDGE pays by reimbursement after the activity is complete and paid in full. The FAQ says claims for most activities open from 1 January 2027. Market Entry Setup also requires a signed engagement letter with an auditor from Enterprise Singapore's panel before you submit the claim, and the audit must be completed before Enterprise Singapore assesses it.

The Market Entry Setup page gives an application outcome time of six weeks. Allow for that before the date you plan to sign with your vendors in Japan.

Singapore side: the tax deduction

The Double Tax Deduction for Internationalisation lets a company deduct 200% of qualifying expenses, so SGD 1 spent gives SGD 2 of deduction. Enterprise Singapore's page says that from Year of Assessment 2027 a company can claim directly with IRAS, without prior approval, when its total DTDi expenses for the year are not more than SGD 400,000. Prior approval is needed above that amount, for setting up an overseas trade office, for an e-commerce campaign, and for a company that enjoys certain tax incentives under the Income Tax Act or the Economic Expansion Incentives Act.

Qualifying activities listed by Enterprise Singapore include market surveys and feasibility studies, overseas market development trips, overseas trade fairs, overseas advertising and promotional campaigns, overseas business development, and investment feasibility or due diligence studies. Some have limits, such as two employees per trip.

The two schemes can be used together. The EDGE FAQ says a company can claim DTDi on the same expenses that EDGE's internationalisation activities support, with the qualifying expenses computed net of the EDGE grant. DTDi can also apply to costs that EDGE does not support, such as travel for a market development trip. Confirm the treatment for your own case with your tax adviser.

Singapore side: the GIA Tokyo programme

Enterprise Singapore's Global Innovation Alliance runs a Tokyo Acceleration Programme, operated by Leave a Nest. Applications are open all year and participants are accepted on a rolling basis.

  • Phase 1, Market Discovery Programme. For tech startups and innovative SMEs at the exploratory stage. It includes lectures, one-to-one sessions to build a hypothesis, and an immersion trip to Japan. It runs twice a year.
  • Phase 2, Business Creation Programme. For companies that have committed to Japan. It includes a business development trip with a minimum of five curated one-to-one business meetings.

The official page does not state fees or funding support, so ask the operator.

Japan side: what JETRO offers

JETRO's support for foreign companies planning to invest in Japan is free in principle, with some parts charged. It includes individual consultation on the market, regulations and incentives, and introductions to judicial scriveners, attorneys, tax accountants and labour consultants.

JETRO also offers a free temporary office for fifty business days in principle, at its Invest Japan Business Support Centers in Tokyo, Yokohama, Nagoya, Osaka, Kobe and Fukuoka. Conditions apply, and an extension carries a tenancy fee.

JETRO's list of national incentives does not show a cash grant for a foreign company setting up a sales subsidiary. The list includes tax measures, financing, regulatory measures and visa routes. One of the visa routes is the startup visa, which JETRO describes as up to two years of status for startup preparation, subject to review by the implementing local government.

Two points follow from the table. Some of these incentives are tied to a sector, such as finance in Tokyo, or to headcount, such as the Kanagawa rent subsidy. And we suggest choosing a location for your customers and hiring first, then checking which incentives apply there.

On the Tokyo and Singapore relationship: the Tokyo Metropolitan Government and Enterprise Singapore signed a memorandum of understanding on 27 October 2026 at SWITCH 2026. Tokyo's announcement describes cooperation on information exchange, startups' international expansion and market access, and links between the two ecosystems. It does not name a grant or a programme.

The order to apply in

  1. Check ownership. If Singaporeans and PRs together hold less than 30% of your company, EDGE is not available and DTDi is the Singapore-side tool to look at.
  2. Scope the work and get quotations. Decide which Market Entry Setup areas you need in Japan, and get a proposal and quotation from each vendor, addressed to your company.
  3. Apply for EDGE before you sign or pay. One application per activity for Japan, through the Business Grants Portal.
  4. Talk to JETRO in parallel. Its consultation and temporary office do not depend on the grant outcome.
  5. Check local incentives before you choose a location. Ask the prefecture or city whether preliminary consultation is required, as Kanagawa's rent subsidy requires.
  6. Keep records for DTDi. Track qualifying expenses from the start, including those EDGE does not support.

Frequently asked questions

Can the EDGE Grant pay for setting up a company in Japan?

Enterprise Singapore's Market Entry Setup activity under the EDGE Grant lists entity incorporation, tax advisory, import and export licences, intellectual property, drafting of agreements and trade credit insurance in a target market. It supports professional service costs at up to 70% for SMEs and up to 50% for other companies, within the SGD 100,000 annual cap. Travel, accommodation and staff costs are not supported.

Who qualifies for the EDGE Grant?

Enterprise Singapore requires a business registered and operating in Singapore with at least 30% ownership, direct or indirect, by Singaporeans or Singapore permanent residents, determined by ultimate individual ownership. For the SME support level, group annual sales turnover must not exceed SGD 100 million or group employment must not exceed 200 workers.

Can we claim the Double Tax Deduction for Internationalisation on costs the EDGE Grant also supports?

Enterprise Singapore's EDGE Grant FAQ says yes, with qualifying expenses for the tax deduction computed net of the EDGE grant. Confirm the treatment for your own case with your tax adviser.

Does Japan give grants to foreign companies that set up there?

There is no single national cash grant listed by JETRO for a foreign company setting up a sales subsidiary. Support includes free services from JETRO, such as a temporary office for up to 50 business days, and local-government incentives that differ by prefecture and city. JETRO's regional pages list, for example, a rent subsidy in Kanagawa and a set-up grant in Fukuoka. Confirm current terms with each government before you plan around them.
