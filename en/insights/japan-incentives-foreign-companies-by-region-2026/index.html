Japan's incentives for foreign companies, by region (2026)

16 November 2026 · Synapse Arrows

About this article: Written by Synapse Arrows, which sells Japan market-entry support to companies from Singapore and the region. The last section describes our own service. The figures below are as listed on JETRO's regional pages and the government sites named at the end, read on 16 November 2026. JETRO's regional pages are not uniformly dated or current (Yokohama's incentive entries, for example, refer to fiscal 2022), and budgets can close during the year. Treat this as a map of what exists, and confirm current terms with each government before you plan around them.

Synapse Arrows is a Singapore-incorporated company whose founder is based in Japan. We help companies from Singapore and the region set up in Japan and work towards their first Japanese customers.

This article is for founders choosing where in Japan to set up. It covers what applies nationwide, what each region lists for foreign companies, and what to check before you apply. For Singapore-side funding, see grants and public support for a Singapore company entering Japan.

The short version
  • Nationwide support includes services, visa routes, tax measures and programme-specific grants. Qualifying new entrants can ask JETRO about its free temporary offices.
  • The local incentives below include support for registration costs, office rent, and visits before you decide.
  • The conditions matter more than the amounts: first entry to Japan, sector, headcount, floor area, and applying before you sign or start.
  • Startup visa applications go through an approved local government or private organisation, so check what is available where you plan to set up.

What applies nationwide

JETRO's free services

JETRO offers foreign companies planning to invest in Japan individual consultation and introductions to judicial scriveners, attorneys, tax accountants and labour consultants. It also offers a free temporary office for fifty business days in principle at its Invest Japan Business Support Centers in Tokyo, Yokohama, Nagoya, Osaka, Kobe and Fukuoka. Conditions apply.

The startup visa

JETRO describes the startup visa as a residential status under the Project for Encouraging Foreign Entrepreneurs to Start Business. It permits startup preparation activities for up to two years, subject to review by the approved implementing organisation. METI's guidance says applications go through an approved local government or private organisation, so check what is available where you plan to set up.

Measures for regional headquarters and R&D

JETRO's list of national incentives also includes a law for multinational enterprises that set up a domestic affiliate for new R&D or headquarters functions. Its measures include faster patent examination and shorter review times for investment notifications and for Certificates of Eligibility. This is aimed at larger investments than a first sales office.

Other regions: general location incentives and startup support

JETRO's pages for Miyagi, Chiba and Saitama list general location incentives and no cash incentive aimed at foreign companies. Sendai and Yokohama list general incentives and the startup visa. Yokohama's page also lists support for overseas companies, including JETRO's free temporary office, described there as the IBSC Kanagawa. A foreign company can still apply for general incentives where it meets the conditions, which are usually tied to investment in fixed assets or to hiring.

Four things to check before you apply

  1. Timing. Nagoya's rent subsidy and Kanagawa's rent subsidy both require an application before the lease or property contract. Ask about timing before you commit to an office.
  2. First entry. Several incentives are for a company entering Japan for the first time. A company that already has a Japanese entity elsewhere may not qualify.
  3. Headcount and space. Rent subsidies come with staff and floor-area conditions that a two-person sales office may not meet.
  4. Budget and year. Local subsidies run on annual budgets that can close early, and terms change from one fiscal year to the next. Japan's fiscal year starts in April.

We suggest choosing a location for your customers and hiring first, then checking which incentives apply there.

Frequently asked questions

Which Japanese cities offer incentives to foreign companies?

JETRO's regional pages list incentives aimed at foreign companies in, among others, Tokyo, Kanagawa, Aichi and Nagoya, Kyoto, Osaka City, Kobe, Hiroshima, Fukuoka, Sapporo and Ibaraki. They range from help with registration costs of a few hundred thousand yen to office rent subsidies of up to JPY 10 million a year. Each has its own conditions, and JETRO's pages are not uniformly dated, so confirm current terms with each government.

What is Japan's startup visa?

JETRO describes it as a residential status under the Project for Encouraging Foreign Entrepreneurs to Start Business. It permits startup preparation activities for up to two years, subject to review by the approved implementing organisation. Applications go through an approved local government or private organisation.

Is there a free office for foreign companies setting up in Japan?

JETRO offers qualifying foreign companies a free temporary office for fifty business days in principle at its centres in Tokyo, Yokohama, Nagoya, Osaka, Kobe and Fukuoka. JETRO's Osaka City page also lists a free serviced office run locally for up to six months. Conditions apply to each.

Should we choose a location in Japan for its incentives?

We suggest choosing a location for your customers and hiring first, then checking which incentives apply there. Several incentives require an application before you sign a lease or start work, so check before you commit.
