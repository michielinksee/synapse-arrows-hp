Does your Japanese subsidiary need a director who lives in Japan?

16 November 2026 · Synapse Arrows

About this article: Written by Synapse Arrows, which sells Japan market-entry support to companies from Singapore and the region, including serving as a Japan-resident director. The last section describes our own service. The rules below come from the official pages listed at the end, read on 16 November 2026. This is general information, not legal or tax advice.

Synapse Arrows is a Singapore-incorporated company whose founder is based in Japan. We help companies from Singapore and the region set up in Japan and work towards their first Japanese customers.

This article is for founders deciding who will sit on the board of their Japanese company. It covers what the law requires, what a company with no director in Japan has to arrange, what the role carries, and five ways to cover it.

The short version
  • A subsidiary (KK or GK) is not required by law to have a representative who lives in Japan. A branch is.
  • A company whose directors all live overseas can be set up. It takes extra steps for the capital payment, signatures and the bank account.
  • A director in Japan carries a director's duties and liability. Decide the scope, authority and protection before the appointment.
  • A non-resident director's pay from a Japanese company is Japan-source income under the National Tax Agency's guidance.

What the law requires

JETRO's comparison of business forms gives the position for each.

FormWho must be appointedMust a representative live in Japan?
Kabushiki kaisha (KK)One or more directors for a KK without a board of directors; three or more for a KK with a board. Whether a board is required depends on the company's structure, not on size aloneNo. The address requirement has not applied to a KK's representative director since 16 March 2015
Godo kaisha (GK)No legally stipulated minimum of executivesNo. The same applies to a GK's representative member
Branch of a foreign companyOne or more representatives in JapanYes. At least one representative must have an address in and be resident in Japan

JETRO lists director terms of one to ten years for a transfer-restricted KK without the specified committees, and two years for a public KK without those committees. Executives of a GK and representatives of a branch have no legally stipulated term.

So for a subsidiary, the question is a practical one. The law allows a board that sits entirely overseas.

What a company with no director in Japan has to arrange

The capital payment

In a KK incorporation, capital is remitted to the account of an incorporator, the representative director or a director at incorporation. JETRO explains that when all of them live overseas, the promoter may delegate receipt of the capital payment to a third party. That route needs a capital-receipt certificate from the representative director, a power of attorney and a copy of the third party's bankbook.

Signatures

JETRO notes that the parent company's affidavits must be attested by a notary in the parent's own country, and that a signature certificate might be needed for a foreign director who lives overseas.

The bank account

JETRO lists the documents a bank asks for, following the Japanese Bankers Association: the certificate of registered matters, the corporate seal registration certificate, identification of the person in charge of the transaction, and a bank seal. Each bank screens on its own criteria and may decline. JETRO's bank-account guidance does not state a resident-director requirement, and individual banks set their own requirements. Ask each bank what it expects before you decide the board.

Time on the ground

JETRO's set-up guide says the local representative will need to reside in Japan on a semi-long-term basis to begin start-up procedures, unless that person already lives there. JETRO presents this as part of the process, not as a legal condition for forming the company.

Visiting without a work status

A director who flies in for short stays should check what the visit allows. JETRO describes temporary visitor status as covering participation in conferences and other meetings and other short-term business activities, and says holders may not engage in working activities, meaning receiving remuneration or operating a business that generates income. Stays are basically within 90, 30 or 15 days. Confirm with an immigration professional how this applies to your director's activities.

Moving a founder to Japan

A foreign founder who relocates to manage the subsidiary needs a status of residence that permits that activity, such as Business Manager, unless the founder already holds a status that allows it. For the Business Manager route, JETRO lists capital of JPY 30 million or more as one of the requirements, and says the office generally must be an exclusive, physical office independent from other companies. JETRO also describes a special measure under which qualifying companies it supports can use certain shared premises. Check the Immigration Services Agency's current criteria for the full list.

What the role carries

Duties and liability

A person validly appointed as a director takes on the duties of that office under the Companies Act. The Act makes directors liable to third parties for damage caused by bad faith or gross negligence in performing their duties (Article 429). Separately, Article 427 lets eligible directors who do not execute business agree limits on their liability to the company, where the articles of incorporation provide for it and the statutory conditions are met. It does not limit the Article 429 liability to third parties. Since 1 March 2021 the Act has also set out rules for indemnification agreements between a company and its officers (Article 430-2) and for directors' and officers' liability insurance (Article 430-3).

Ask your lawyer in Japan how these apply to the board you plan, including to a director who does not run the business day to day.

Authority to sign

JETRO distinguishes directors from the representative director. Who can bind the company depends on how the company is organised and who is appointed to represent it. Decide this on purpose, in the articles and the appointment documents, along with who holds the company seal.

Tax on a director's pay

JETRO describes a resident as a person with a domicile in Japan, or a residence in Japan for one year or more. Salary paid in Japan to a resident is subject to withholding.

For a non-resident, the National Tax Agency lists remuneration for work carried out in Japan as Japan-source income, and includes work carried out outside Japan by a person acting as a director of a domestic corporation. For a non-resident with no permanent establishment, this income is withheld at 20.42%, and the payer must withhold. The agency notes that a tax treaty may reduce the rate. Confirm the treatment of your directors with a tax adviser.

A public address

The address of a KK's representative director is a registered matter. Under a Ministry of Justice measure in force since 1 October 2024, a KK can ask, together with a registration application, for that address to be shown only as far as the municipality on certificates. Documents are required, and the request cannot be made on its own for a representative who is already registered.

Questions to settle before you appoint an outside director

  1. Representative or not. Will this person represent the company, or sit on the board without representing it?
  2. Signing authority and the seal. What may this person sign alone, what needs your approval, and who keeps the company seal and the bank token?
  3. Scope of work. Is the role limited to the board, or does it include bank meetings, the lease, hiring and customer meetings?
  4. Protection. Is there an indemnification agreement and directors' and officers' insurance, and has your lawyer confirmed how they are approved under the Companies Act?
  5. Pay and tax. How is the remuneration set, and who handles withholding and filings?
  6. The end of the role. When does it end, who replaces this person, and who files the change of registration?

Frequently asked questions

Does a Japanese subsidiary need a director who lives in Japan?

Not by law. JETRO states that since 16 March 2015 the address requirement does not apply to the representative director of a kabushiki kaisha (KK) or the representative member of a godo kaisha (GK). A branch is different: at least one representative of a branch must have an address in and be resident in Japan.

Can we set up a Japanese company when every director lives overseas?

JETRO describes how. When the promoter, the representative director and the directors at incorporation all live overseas, the promoter may delegate receipt of the capital payment to a third party, and a signature certificate may be needed for a director living overseas. Banks screen account applications on their own criteria and may decline, so plan the bank account early.

How is a non-resident director's pay from a Japanese company taxed?

The National Tax Agency lists remuneration for work as a director of a domestic corporation, including work carried out outside Japan, as Japan-source income. For a non-resident with no permanent establishment, it is withheld at 20.42%. A tax treaty may change the result, so confirm with a tax adviser.

Is a director's home address public in Japan?

The address of a KK's representative director is a registered matter. Since 1 October 2024 a KK can ask, together with a registration application, for the address to be shown only as far as the municipality on certificates. Conditions and documents apply, and the request cannot be made on its own for a representative who is already registered.
