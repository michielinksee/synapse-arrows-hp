Research and analysis

We collect and analyse publicly available information: quantitative market and industry research, cross-checks of public directories and databases, and measurements of how AI assistants describe and recommend businesses. Each report sets out its scope, the dates the data was collected, the method and the limitations.

FOR RECIPIENTS

If you have received a report from us

We sometimes cross-check and count publicly available information, and send the findings to the organisation that publishes or maintains that information, or to the businesses concerned. If you received a report you did not ask for, this is how we handle it.

  • We study publicly available information. We do not collect non-public transaction information or internal documents.
  • We collect by machine. We control the frequency and volume of requests to limit the load on the sites we read.
  • The main report contains no information that would identify an individual business, including its name, address or URL. Business-specific findings are sent as a separate appendix to the organisation that publishes or maintains the information, limited to what is needed to check them.
  • We do not publish information that would identify an individual business — its name, address or URL, or the findings for an individual record. You are not being asked to decide whether it may be published.
  • We may publish the aggregate results as our own research — in a press release, in an interview, or in Insights on this site — but never in a form that identifies an individual business.
  • There is no charge for the report. Reading it and replying are both optional, and you do not need to buy any service.

For questions or corrections about the report, or to stop receiving future reports, write to contact@synapse-arrows.com.

We also publish research articles in Insights on this site. You can use the same address to discuss corrections to, or removal of, an Insights article.

HOW WE WORK

What every report states

Each report sets out the following.

  • The scope and the date: how many records, collected when.
  • The method: the sources, when they were collected, and how they were cross-checked and counted, so the method can be examined.
  • What we confirmed and what we could not confirm, kept apart, with the second never written as the first.
  • The limits, in a section of their own, so the reader can judge what the result does not mean.
  • That we draw no conclusion about the accuracy of a listing, or about a business's operating status, from a mismatch alone.
  • That we do not speculate about causes or circumstances, and keep observed facts apart from the conclusions those facts support.
